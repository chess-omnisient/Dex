Re-identification protection is an automatic check the Omnisient Platform runs on datasets you own when you add them to a project. It removes or rounds values that are distinctive enough to single out an individual person, which can leave a dataset with fewer records or columns than you uploaded.

If an overlap or audience count looks smaller than you calculated independently, this is usually re-identification protection at work.

Why This Happens

A project matches your data with your partner's data. Each row in the result holds information from both organizations about a customer.

Suppose one customer is the only person in your file with an account balance of R83,120. If that exact balance also appears in the project, you can look it up in your own records and see whose row it is. At that point you are no longer looking at an anonymous row. You are looking at everything your partner contributed about a named customer of yours, which is not what they agreed to share.

Removing and rounding one-of-a-kind values like that account balance leaves nothing to look up. Your partner cannot do the same in the other direction, because they have no customer list of yours to match against. This is why the protection is applied to the data of whoever is doing the analysis.

💡 Re-identification risk is the chance that an individual person can be picked out of an anonymized dataset. Some values look harmless on their own but are so specific to one person that they act like a fingerprint, for example an account balance that only one customer has ever held.

What Changes in Your Data

When you add a dataset you own to a project, the Omnisient Platform assesses it for re-identification risk and adjusts what it finds before the data becomes visible inside the project. Any one dataset may be subject to some or all of the following.

Adjustment

What you see in the project

Identifying columns are removed

Your customer identifier column, platform-generated reference columns (iURN, if applicable), and any duplicate-identifier columns are always removed. Any column distinctive enough to single out individuals on its own is also removed.
​
Fewer columns.

Detailed values are rounded

Where a numeric column carries some risk but does not need removing, its values are rounded so unusual values no longer stand out.
​
Same records, less precise values.

One-of-a-kind records are removed

Records that remain unique after the adjustments above are removed.
​
Fewer records.

The difference between what you uploaded and what appears in the project is the combined result of these adjustments.

How to Check What Happened to Your Data

Two report tables record what re-identification protection did to a dataset, and you can query them in SQLPad. Both exist only for datasets you own. You will not see them for a partner's shared dataset.

Finding the tables

  1. Open the project's tables in SQLPad.

  2. Find the table you need in the table list and copy its name. Each one is prefixed with your dataset name. For example, the removal report for a dataset named CustomerFile is CustomerFile_KANONREPORT.

  3. Query it with SELECT *, as shown in each section below.

💡 A report can come back with no rows at all. That means no column in your dataset was distinctive enough to need adjusting, so nothing was removed or rounded. This is a normal result.

Records removed

SELECT * FROM <your_dataset_name>_KANONREPORT

Each row is a column that was checked.

Column

What it tells you

ColumnName

The column in your dataset that was checked.

RecordsDropped

How many records were removed because of that column. A value of 0 means the column was checked but caused no removals.

ColsRemaining

Not needed for reviewing what changed in your dataset. You can ignore this column.

Values rounded 

SELECT * FROM <your_dataset_name>_Columns_To_Round

Each row is a numeric column and the rounding applied to it. Rounding a column changes how precise its values are. It does not remove records, so a column can appear here having dropped nothing.

Column

What it tells you

ColumnName

The numeric column that was assessed.

RoundingValue

The rounding applied to that column. A value of 0 means the column was left as it was.

⚠️ These counts will not always tie back exactly to your original file. If something looks more seriously wrong, please contact support.

How to Keep More of Your Data

Banding replaces a precise value with the range it falls into, so an age of 37 becomes 35-44 and a salary of R48,230 becomes R40,000-R59,999. Precise values are what make a record distinctive, so banding your data before you upload it means fewer columns are removed and fewer records are dropped. Most analysis cares about "high earner in their 40s" rather than the exact rand and the exact birthday, so banding usually costs less than it appears to.

Three things to do before you upload:

  • Band your most detailed columns. Dates, exact amounts, precise timestamps, and full postcodes are the usual causes of a large drop.

  • Collapse the extremes. Very high values are rare enough to stand out on their own, so group them into an open-ended top band such as R1m+ rather than leaving the tail exposed.

  • Upload only the columns your analysis needs. A column you never intended to analyze can still hold unique values that cause records to be removed. Leaving it out avoids losing records to a field that was contributing nothing to your results.

Worked example: a bank customer file

Uploaded as

Band it as

Date of birth: 1987-04-12

Age band: 35-44

Postcode: 7708

Region: Cape Town Metro

Monthly income: R52,400

Income band: R40,000-R59,999

Balance: R83,120

Balance band: R50,000-R99,999

A date of birth is close to unique on its own, and so is an exact balance. As bands, these fields still support life-stage and catchment analysis, and far fewer records stand out as distinctive enough to be removed.

Worked example: a retailer transaction file

Uploaded as

Band it as

Timestamp: 2026-08-14 18:42:07

Day part: Weekday evening

Store: Claremont

Region: Cape Town Metro

Basket value: R1,247.83

Basket band: R1,000-R1,499

Visits per month: 31

Visits band: 15+

A timestamp to the second and a basket value to the cent can each be unique to a customer. As a day part and a spend band, they still carry the shopping-mission signal merchandising uses, and far fewer records stand out as distinctive.

⚠️ Banding reduces the likelihood our re-identification process removes rows and columns from your dataset. However, a dataset that is still distinctive after banding will still be impacted by our processes.
