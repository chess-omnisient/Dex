# CS Intake Form – Standard Operating Procedure

## 1. Introduction

The CS intake form is how sales and data product teams request work from the customer success (CS) team. It's designed to capture enough detail up front – client, region, data seller, urgency, revenue, stakeholders – so CS can action the request onto the appropriate sprint board without needing to chase down missing information.

**Training:** Recorded walkthrough of the form, covering each field and how to fill it in for a sample request (an ABSA overlap report).

---

## 2. Step-by-Step Guide: Filling Out the Form

### Step 1 – Request name
Give the request a short, descriptive name. Example: "ABSA Overlap Report Test."

### Step 2 – Department
Select which department the request is coming from: **sales** or **data product**.

### Step 3 – Sales owner
Select the sales owner for this request – currently either [[Chris_Brown|Chris]] or [[Anton_Grutzmacher|Anton]].

### Step 4 – Requester
Enter the person making the request (defaults to yourself).

### Step 5 – Client
Select the client this request is for (e.g. ABSA).

### Step 6 – Region
Select the region: South Africa, US, MENA, UK, Brazil, or APAC.

### Step 7 – Data seller
Select the relevant data seller from the dropdown (e.g. ShopRite).

### Step 8 – Datasets (optional)
Not required, but adding relevant dataset names/IDs helps the CS team going forward. Example: "ABSA Dataset 012, ShopRite Dataset 86."

### Step 9 – Request type
Choose the category from the info block: managed services, platform support, sales and growth support, or data product growth.

### Step 10 – Type of work
Choose the specific work type: overlap, POC, contracted, or ad hoc request.

### Step 11 – Task outcome
Describe the desired result and flag any potential risks. Example: "ABSA requested an overlap. No risks."

### Step 12 – Task description
Give a short summary of what needs to be delivered. Example: "Need an overlap report, Power BI, on the ABSA profile."

### Step 13 – Revenue (optional)
If there's revenue linked to the opportunity, enter the amount and, optionally, when it's expected to land. This helps CS assess priority. Example: 1 million rand, expected next Friday.

### Step 14 – Urgency
Select how urgent the task is: this week, next week, next sprint, or not urgent. Base this on the due date – e.g. a due date of 25 September maps to "next sprint."

### Step 15 – Priority
Select urgent, high, normal, or low.

### Step 16 – Frequency
Select once-off or recurring.

### Step 17 – Internal stakeholders
List anyone on the Omnisient team the requester depends on to complete the work – e.g. a specific person who needs to supply information.

### Step 18 – Attachments
Upload any relevant files, then click submit.

---

## 3. Standard Operating Procedure

**Trigger:** This is a request-triggered process, not a recurring check – there's no standing daily/weekly review. Submitting the form is what kicks off the process.

**On submission:**
1. The request pings the CS team.
2. CS actions it onto the appropriate sprint board based on the urgency, priority, and request type fields.

**Escalation:**
- If a submission needs help or something's unclear, escalate to the CoE – [[Aimee_Malan|Aimee]] or Chess.
